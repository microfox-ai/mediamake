# Music, lyric videos, song edits, AMVs

The skill's first playbook: the user's 13 anime and motivation edit references (ENDLESS BLADE,
YOU'RE WRONG, RELEASE THE BEAST, ITACHI, I CAN, JUST GRIND, MONSTER OF GOD, URAHARA, GOAT,
UNSTOPPABLE, ATOMIC, MONSTER) and every round in the feedback log up to November 2026. Reworked on
2026-10-31 after the user said ours were "not up to the mark" beside real ones: side by side, ours
were frames of the video with a small word; theirs share the eight points below.

## What makes people click here (every thumbnail of this kind passes these)

1. **One giant word.** The hero word spans 60–100 % of the frame width (≥ 770 px of 1280) or is at
   least a third of its height, in a heavy or striking face. At 25–35 % it reads as a caption.
2. **A big, lit subject.** The face or figure fills at least half the frame height; the face is lit
   (a rim of light, glowing eyes, a hard key) against darkness, close enough that the expression
   reads at 168 px wide.
3. **A simplified background.** Dark, blurred, one flat field or one graphic shape (a red moon,
   lightning, rain). Never the video's interface (its wave, lyric type, rings) unless that element
   is the hook. Recompose: plate + cutout, blur or darken the rest.
4. **One dominant hue and one accent.** A near-monochrome picture and one saturated accent (the
   word, the eyes, a moon). Red word on a red picture has no accent: grade the picture away first.
5. **The word and the subject interlock.** The subject in front of part of the word (still
   readable), the word across the lower third over the body, or split either side of the head.
6. **Three tiers of type, sparingly.** A small tracked kicker, the giant word, a sub in a
   contrasting voice. Most references use two tiers; none more than three.
7. **A question in the picture.** A gaze into the lens, a masked or half-hidden face, glowing eyes,
   a clenched fist, a weapon.
8. **Readable in the feed.** At 168×94 the word and the face still read.

## Archetypes for the board

Giant word behind the subject (GOAT, URAHARA) · word across the lower third over a big figure
(MONSTER OF GOD, UNSTOPPABLE) · extreme close-up of the eyes with a side word (ENDLESS BLADE) ·
subject on a flat field or one shape (ITACHI's moon) · monochrome with one accent (YOU'RE WRONG,
JUST GRIND) · energy effects around the subject (RELEASE THE BEAST) · a split or mirrored
composition · a vertical title up one edge for a one-word song title (≤ 8 letters) · a lyric
question stacked beside a close-up · a giant numeral for a rank in the name · for an edit, its own
graphic moments rebuilt (title card, word pops, signature effect) in its palette and fonts.

## Rules learned in the rounds (details in SKILL.md's feedback log)

- **Words behind the subject** (`behind`): the word must still read. Put it at head and neck
  height, hide a third at most, prefer 6+ letter words people can complete. A close-up hides the
  middle of a centred word (a head is ~500 px wide): close-ups get side layouts, only mid shots
  keep the word behind. When the user asks for it bigger behind the figure, do it.
- **Text on the calm side** (`side`), stacked, centred top to bottom with equal gaps, each line its
  own width-fit text layer when the lines must share one width.
- **A required title** becomes the hero with a hierarchy (KINGDOM big, "YOU CAN / HAVE THE" small).
- **Frames**: the sharpest frame of the best source file, checked for the moment; never an upscale.
- **An accent once**, not as a glow over everything; vignette and grain go under the type.
- **Fire or picture-filled letters** on a same-hued picture need lifted exposure inside the
  letters, a black outline and a drop shadow.
- **Deity and devotional posters**: the title on the calm side below the top ornament, one
  saffron word; words keep their case (`upper: false`).
- **No-text sets** for a character poster may carry the character's name as art behind them.
- Watermarks: two channels on opposite top corners, each logo black or white per picture.
