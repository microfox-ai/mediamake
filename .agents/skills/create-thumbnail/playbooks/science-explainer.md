# Science and explainers

Channels read: @veritasium, @kurzgesagt, @MarkRober, @3blue1brown, @SteveMould
(`mm thumbs inspire science-explainer @veritasium @kurzgesagt @MarkRober @3blue1brown @SteveMould`).

## What the hits share

- **One curious thing**: an object (15 of 40), a diagram (7), a drawn character (4) or the words
  themselves (6). A face is on only 9 of 40; on the misses it is on 5 of 15.
- **An arrow or circle on the odd detail** (13 of 40): the picture points at its own question.
- **Words are optional**: 16 of 40 have none; with words the median is 2 and the line is giant.
- **Dark grounds lead** (17), then a real photo (8), then white (4).

## The kit (gather before the board)

- the real object or phenomenon, cut out clean; a hand for scale
- public science pictures come free: NASA, ESA and museum photos on Commons are public domain or
  CC0 (`mm thumbs pic`); the object's museum twin cuts out cleaner than the field photo
- the experiment's best frame; a simple diagram drawn for the thumbnail
- arrows and rings as drawn layers, in the accent colour

## Archetypes for the board

1. **A question over a dark photo** (Veritasium): a short sentence in a serif, sentence case, with
   one red underline or arrow ("This shouldn't exist.", "How was this allowed?").
2. **Banner over vector art** (Kurzgesagt): 1–3 caps words across the top, one drawn subject in
   saturated flat colour on a dark ground, a white arrow.
3. **Object on a clean ground + stacked words** (Steve Mould): the real object large on white or
   one soft colour, a hand for scale, 2–4 condensed caps words, arrows drawn on the motion.
4. **Wordless stunt photo** (Mark Rober): one saturated real scene, a red arrow or circle, nothing
   else. Needs a picture that is itself the experiment.
5. **Diagram on black** (3Blue1Brown): thin glowing lines, a few serif words or none.
6. **Compare**: three of a thing with a label each and the odd one marked ("EASY · EASY · ALMOST
   IMPOSSIBLE"), or a before / after pair with the years.
7. **One word on the phenomenon**: a single giant word over a texture of the thing (ENTROPY).

## Type

Two voices work here that music thumbnails never use: a **serif in sentence case** for a question
(6 of the hits), and a clean heavy sans for labels. Effects are nearly absent on hits (a
highlighted word on 3); the older stroked caps are on the misses (6 of 15). Fonts: SKILL.md,
"editorial, documentary" and "clean, tech".

## Colour and ground

A saturated pair on black (blue and orange, cyan and yellow), or flat colour on white. The subject
separates by brightness alone: glowing on black, flat on white. Red is the arrow.

## Title + thumbnail

Three ways, all on hits: no words (16), words that add a twist the title lacks (14: "Moon Crashes
into Earth" + NOT THIS…), or the question repeated with the picture as its proof (10: "Can water
solve a maze?"). Never facts on the thumbnail: it asks, the video answers.

## Hits vs misses

Nothing visual separates them inside a channel. Misses are the channel's Q&As, announcements and
personal videos, and the only thumbnails with a big face and a logo.

## Build notes

- The object must be real and odd: a cutout on a `fill`, a hand kept in when there is one.
- Arrows and circles are `shape` layers (`arrow`, `ring`) in the one accent colour, thick enough
  to read at 168 px (≥ 10 px on 1280).
- Diagrams are drawn for the thumbnail (a job `custom/` component or an SVG `image`), far simpler
  than the video's own: three to five elements.
