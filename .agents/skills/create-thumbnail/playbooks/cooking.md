# Cooking, food

Channels read: @JoshuaWeissman, @NickDiGiovanni, @EthanChlebowski, @aragusea, @bonappetit
(`mm thumbs inspire cooking @JoshuaWeissman @NickDiGiovanni @EthanChlebowski @aragusea @bonappetit`).

## What the hits share

- **Food big and glossy**, with a person: a face is on 34 of 40, yet the hero is spread evenly
  between the food (10), an object (9), the face (9) and the words (8). The cook presents the food;
  the food is never small.
- **Held toward the lens** (21 of 40): a plate, a pan, an ingredient in each hand.
- **Words are optional**: 18 have none; median 2; giant when present (10). Heavy sans caps with a
  soft shadow (12) or on a plate (7).
- **Real light**: a natural photo on 24 of 40, bright. Dark grounds are rare.
- The gap in how fast it reads is the widest after documentary (4.6 against 3.5).

## Three sub-kinds, three looks

**Challenge and entertainment** (Joshua Weissman, Nick DiGiovanni)
1. **Cook behind a spread**: the host centred behind a table of dishes, one giant word either side
   of the head (EVERY | STATE).
2. **Triptych**: three panels on one flat colour, the same cook holding a different creation in each.
3. **Face + a giant novelty**, no words: a shocked face beside an absurd dish, an animal, a guest.

**Comparison and food science** (Ethan Chlebowski)
4. **Two things, two price plates**: the face between a cheap and a dear version, a price or a
   label on a plate over each (`$2` / `$9`; HOME COOK / CHEF), sometimes calories.
5. **Science shelf**: a title plate (GARLIC SCIENCE) over a row of the forms compared, each labelled.
6. **A claim beside the ingredient**: a clean ground, the raw thing huge, one sentence
   ("You've been lied to.").

**Recipe and kitchen documentary** (Adam Ragusea, Bon Appétit)
7. **Dish close-up + a top line**: the food fills the frame, 2–3 white caps words across the top
   (ONIONS 101, SUNDAY SAUCE).
8. **Dish and its cook at work**: the plate large in the foreground, the chef behind it mid-task,
   no words, a small logo bottom left.

## Type

Heavy sans caps in white with a shadow; plates for prices (green for cheap, red for dear). A
warmer voice suits recipe channels: a soft serif or a rounded face for the dish's name. Fonts:
SKILL.md, "food, lifestyle".

## Colour and ground

Warm food against a cool ground (grey, blue, white): the red, orange and green of the dish is the
accent. Nick DiGiovanni's flat blue field is the brightest version. Almost no outlines or glows:
separation is by size, saturation and a blurred kitchen.

## Title + thumbnail

The picture shows the dish or the contest; the title asks the question. Prices and labels on the
thumbnail are the proof the title promises ("Is Parmigiano Reggiano actually worth it?" + `$4.90`
/ `$20.99`). Recipe channels repeat the dish's name, and that is right for search.

## Hits vs misses

Misses are the talk and vlog videos (a microphone, a thin title on a plain ground, time-of-day
panels), a guest at a desk with no food, four people with no dish.

## Tried in the rounds

Iteration 7, "The Pizza Named After a Queen" (2026-11-15, run autonomously; not yet reviewed):
real Neapolitan Margherita photos, the Brandi plaque, the ingredients, and Queen Margherita's
portrait as the host. Own checks: a historical host can't hold the food to the lens, so the dish
stays the biggest thing and the host presents from the side, cropped to head and shoulders (a
full-length portrait made a tiny host); a disputed origin story is asked, never stated (`TRUE?`,
`NAMED AFTER HER?`); `3 INGREDIENTS, / 1 FLAG.` on the flag's colours is the kind's own claim device.

## Build notes

- Food pictures must be appetising first: sharp, lit from the side or back, saturated but true.
  Raise `exposure` and saturation on the dish only (a cutout over its own blurred photo).
- A price plate is a `text` layer with `plate`; keep each under a fifth of the frame width.
