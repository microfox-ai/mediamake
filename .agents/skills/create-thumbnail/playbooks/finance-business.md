# Finance, business

Channels read: @GrahamStephan, @AndreiJikh, @HowMoneyWorks, @ThePlainBagel, @MagnatesMedia
(`mm thumbs inspire finance-business @GrahamStephan @AndreiJikh @HowMoneyWorks @ThePlainBagel @MagnatesMedia`).

## What the hits share

- **A worried face leads** (25 of 40; serious on 19): the host with a hand on the face or palms
  together, next to the person or thing the news is about.
- **A recognisable mark**: a company's logo on 23 of 40, a flag on 10.
- **Giant words when there are words** (22 giant): median 2, but this is the one kind where a long
  line can win (How Money Works runs 6–10 words). 10 have none.
- **A busy composite in alarm colours** (30 of 40): red, yellow, orange, with flames and charts.
- **A fixed frame** on 11 (a news lower third).

## The kit (gather before the board)

- the host's worried or serious face, cut out
- the person or company in the news: press portrait, official logo (`thumbs logo`)
- the figure, a chart's real shape, a flag; a news lower third as layers

## Archetypes for the board

1. **News lower third** (Graham Stephan, Andrei Jikh): the host beside a figure in the news, and
   across the bottom a TV-style banner with 2–5 caps words ("STOCKS WILL RISE 15%").
2. **Host between two powers**: the face in the centre, a leader or flag each side, gold, charts.
3. **A sentence as the picture** (How Money Works): a full question or claim in tall condensed caps
   filling the top and bottom of a photo or a grid, one object between the lines.
4. **Host + the thing + a figure** (The Plain Bagel): an outlined cutout of the host, the house or
   bank or bubble, and one number with an arrow (`$5.5M →`).
5. **The brand as the hero** (MagnatesMedia): one company logo or mascot alone in fire or neon;
   the logo is the face.
6. **A person and a verdict**: a tycoon's portrait with DARK TRUTH or LEVELS OF WEALTH in red or gold.
7. **The number is the picture**: `$31.4 TRILLION` across the frame over the building it belongs to.

## Type

Heavy or tall condensed sans, caps, white with a black outline or on a red or yellow plate. A
strike-through on a word (~~DEBT~~) and a red underline are this kind's own devices. Fonts:
SKILL.md, "news, finance" and "tall, punchy".

## Colour and ground

Red and yellow mean crisis, green means money. White grounds are rare and mostly on misses.
Cutouts carry a white outline or a red glow.

## Title + thumbnail

The words give a verdict the title only hints at (26 complement, 4 repeat): the title asks what
happened to silver, the banner says SILVER BANNED ON JAN 1. Any figure or claim on the thumbnail
must be true to the video: a made-up headline is a policy risk, not a style.

## Hits vs misses

Nothing visible on the template channels: the same banner, pose and colours win and lose. Misses
elsewhere are announcements, cartoons, a plain sentence card: no concrete subject.

## Build notes

- A lower third is two `fill` bars and two `text` layers; keep it above YouTube's progress bar
  (bottom ~24 px) and clear of the timestamp corner.
- Real people in the news are the story's subject: use press or the user's own pictures, cut out
  cleanly, never a generated likeness.
- A company's logo as the hero: the official mark, whole, then the treatment (glow, fire) around
  it, not on it.
